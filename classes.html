Courses

I enjoy teaching data analysis by working with natural language. Here are the courses and seminars I've taught, with materials where available. Feel free to reach out if you want to learn more.

Ling 2000: Introduction to Linguistics, The Ohio State University

Linguistic analysis Working with language data

Taught an introductory undergraduate course that introduces students to the study of language as a system of human communication. The course was a mix of lecturing and hands-on problem solving such as transcribing speech, building syntactic trees and working through data from unfamiliar languages.

Ling 3701: Language and Mind, The Ohio State University

Experimental methods Statistics Hypothesis testing

Lecturer for 30+ student undergraduate course. I designed the syllabus and weekly decks, which served as an introduction to psycholinguists. During the course we answered four fundamental questions: What does one know when one knows a language? How do we perceive language? How do our brains decide what it is that we're hearing? How do interpret strings of sounds into meaningful sentences and conversations? How do babies learn language? How do adults learn a foreign language? Students finished the course with designing a small research project of their own.

Advanced Syntax: Island Effects, Stuttgart University

Critical reading Evaluating theories Data analysis

Advanced Semantics: Anaphora, Stuttgart University

Critical reading Evaluating theories Argumentation

I designed and taught an advanced undergraduate seminar on anaphora: how pronouns and similar expressions find the person or thing they refer to. In "Anna said she was tired" she can mean Anna or someone else. In Anna admires herself, herself can only mean Anna. Each week we read research articles on the rules behind these patters and discussed howe well each analysis fit the data. The students finished the course by designing an experiment aimed at testing one of the existing theories of Anaphora resolution.ibe

Advanced Linguistics: Interrogatives, Stuttgart University

Critical reading Syntax-semantics interface Prosodic analysis

I designed and taught an advanced undergraduate seminar on interrogatives: how they sound, what is their structure and their meaning. Compare "Did Anna leave?", "Anna left" and "Who left?": they differ in word order, melody and what kind of answer they expect. Each week I taught a lecture focusing on one of these three angles and discussed how the analyses fit together.

Syntactic Theory, Stuttgart University

Syntactic analysis Tree diagrams Hypothesis testing

I designed and taught an introductory course on syntactic theory: how words combine into phrases and sentences, and why some combinations work while others don't. The course consisted of weekly lectures with exercises during which the students had to develop syntactic analyses for novel data.

Semantic Theory, Stuttgart University

Propositional Logic First-order Logic Formal reasoning

I co-designed and co-taught an introductory course on semantic theory: how the meaning of a sentence is built from the meaning of its parts. We used propositional and first-order logic to state meanings precisely, then looked at three kinds of information a sentence may carry. For example, Anna stopped smoking entails that she doesn't smoke now and it presupposes that she used to. The course consisted of weekly lectures with exercises during which the students had to develop semantic analyses for novel data.
